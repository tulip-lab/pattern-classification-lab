# Frontier AI through Pattern Classification

## Task

Investigate one approved frontier-AI topic through the lens of Pattern Classification. Formulate a testable question, connect it to the course, and compare the frontier method with a credible simpler baseline using inspectable evidence.

Choose one topic from the [topic catalogue](topics.md). Several groups may use the same topic; the quality of each group's question, experiment, and evidence matters more than topic novelty.

## What your investigation must show

Your presentation and supporting materials must:

1. define the inputs, labels or actions, assumptions, loss, and evaluation data for a classification or classification-supported decision problem;
2. explain the connection to at least one module from `M02`–`M11`;
3. state a testable research question about the selected frontier method;
4. compare it with a credible simpler baseline under comparable information and resource budgets;
5. use held-out cases and appropriate quantitative measures;
6. analyse errors, uncertainty, limitations, and at least one responsible-use risk; and
7. distinguish experimental evidence from demonstrations, vendor claims, and speculation.

A literature survey, product demonstration, or untested architecture proposal does not satisfy the task by itself.

## Practical evidence

Use a public dataset, a safe synthetic dataset, or a small public benchmark. Retain enough code, parameters, prompts, and environment information for the comparison to be inspected and reproduced without credentials.

Where feasible, use at least 50 held-out examples or tasks and repeat stochastic experiments with at least three seeds or independent runs. If this is impractical, justify the smaller design and report the resulting uncertainty. Use at least four scholarly sources, including at least two recent primary research papers.

Privacy and security experiments must use public benchmarks, local sandboxes, or synthetic records. Never test live services, other people's accounts, or real confidential data.

## Presentation format

The presentation is 20 minutes, followed by 5 minutes of questions. At least 5 of the 20 presentation minutes must be an inspectable code or algorithm demonstration.

Recommended structure:

| Time | Focus |
| ---: | --- |
| 2 min | Problem, research question, classes or actions |
| 3 min | Course connection, assumptions, mathematical idea |
| 3 min | Frontier method, baseline, and research evidence |
| **at least 5 min** | **Code or algorithm demonstration with concrete inputs and visible outputs** |
| 4 min | Protocol, comparative results, and reproducibility |
| 2 min | Errors, uncertainty, cost, risks, and limitations |
| 1 min | Direct conclusion and next step |

Static screenshots, a results table, pseudocode read aloud, or a product video do not satisfy the demonstration requirement on their own. A locally reproducible notebook plus an inspectable recording is acceptable when live execution would be unreliable.

## Reusable deliverables

Prepare:

- presentation slides in PDF;
- runnable or inspectable demonstration code with a completed [Demo README](templates/Demo-README.md) inside the demonstration folder;
- a concise record of the experiment configuration and AI/tool use;
- a complete reference list; and
- a contribution statement when the work is completed by a group.

Your current offering page defines group size, weight, dates, exact filenames, submission route, and institutional policy.

## AI and external tools

AI tools may support literature discovery, brainstorming, translation, coding, and editing, or may be part of the investigated method. Verify every citation, calculation, factual claim, and generated result. Record the tool, purpose, relevant configuration or prompt, affected material, and verification. Do not expose credentials, personal data, restricted material, or paid-account details.

## Evaluation focus

The teaching team will look for:

- clear problem framing and a genuine Pattern Classification connection;
- technical accuracy and appropriate primary research;
- a fair, reproducible comparison;
- suitable measures of quality, uncertainty, cost, and failure;
- responsible and critical analysis; and
- clear communication, a substantive demonstration, and defensible answers.

Method size, price, complexity, or fashion does not earn credit by itself. The current offering supplies any formal rubric or mark allocation.
